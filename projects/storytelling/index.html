AI-Enhanced Interactive Storytelling

Supporting executive functions and cultural learning through participatory design

Context
M.Sc. thesis in Cognitive Science, Umeå University, 2025
Supervisor
Dr. Arzu Güneysu, Department of Computer Science
Methods
Participatory design, semi-structured interviews, reflexive thematic analysis, usability testing, high-fidelity prototyping
Output
Peer-reviewed poster, HAI ’25 (ACM) · doi.org/10.1145/3765766.3765806

Try the prototype

A short walkthrough of the high-fidelity prototype. Click the highlighted areas as a child would, or use Next. On the landscape screen you can choose between two paths.

Screens come from the Figma prototype. In the prototype, the AI's lines are scripted. Keyboard: ← and → also move between screens.

What it is

An interactive digital narrative for children to play together with a parent. The child picks a cultural world (for example a Swedish Midsommar meadow) and fills it with characters, objects, animals and weather. Then the child co-creates a story with an AI storyteller.

The story is told in turns. The AI opens with a small mystery, and the child continues it. When the child gets stuck, a help button gives a hint instead of an answer. The story ends with tasks that ask the child to bring in a memory or a story from their own family, so the play becomes a conversation between parent and child.

Why

The goal was to support children's executive functions and cultural learning through parent–child interaction. Turn-taking, deciding what happens next, asking for help and returning to the task all ask the child to plan and keep the story in mind. The cultural worlds and the family-memory tasks connect the story to the child's own background.

How it was made

The prototype was co-designed through participatory design. It was shaped with semi-structured interviews and analysed with reflexive thematic analysis. It was then built as a high-fidelity prototype in Figma and evaluated through usability testing. The work was published as a peer-reviewed poster at the 13th International Conference on Human-Agent Interaction (HAI ’25).
